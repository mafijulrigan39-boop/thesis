## 4. EU legal and policy context (17 November 2026)

1. UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/81/8, adopted 29 October 2026, extended Bangladesh's preparatory period before LDC graduation to **24 November 2029**. This corrects the earlier assumption that the 2026 graduation date remained operative.
2. Regulation (EU) 2026/1395 applies from 1 January 2027. The regulation includes a three-year transitional arrangement for former LDC/EBA beneficiaries after the country ceases to meet LDC criteria, subject to the applicable Commission process. Do not assign a definitive post-EBA date without checking the relevant implementing/status act.
3. The new scheme's Article 33 and Annex IV identify Section S-11b (HS Chapters 61, 62 and 63) for a special safeguard. The 37% trigger is calculated as one beneficiary country's section imports divided by **all GSP-beneficiary countries' imports** in the same section; the 6% exception uses the denominator of **total EU imports from all origins** in the section. These denominators are different. EBA beneficiaries are exempt while still receiving EBA. GSP+ eligibility is not automatic.
4. This means the legal ratio cannot be inferred from Bangladesh's 21.37% share of HS61+62 imports from all origins. The complete calculation requires Chapter 63, all relevant origin countries, year-specific GSP beneficiary status, scheme status and tariff preferences.

## 5. Tariff data design and limitations

- **TARIC:** Official EU database integrates third-country duty, tariff preferences, quotas, trade-defence measures, nomenclature, and country codes. Its raw data are freely available in Excel; daily updated. Use tariff-line-level codes, origin, measure type, additional code, validity from/to, and compound/specific components.
- **WITS / UNCTAD TRAINS:** Public tariff tables/API provide MFN and preferential rates harmonised at HS6, with tariff type, number of national tariff lines, nomenclature revision and exclusion fields. WITS pages currently indicate EU TRAINS coverage up to 2022/2023 in observed data-availability tables; do not assume 2024–2026 historical values exist there. It may be useful as a historical cross-check, not as a substitute for current TARIC CN8/CN10 lines.
- Preference utilisation and preference eligibility are distinct. Tariff scheme status is not evidence that imports actually claimed the preference.
- Rules of origin and product-specific rules are legally technical: use the relevant scheme annex and CN/TARIC classifications, and preserve effective-date ranges.
- Do not collapse mixed, specific or compound duties into a single ad valorem percentage unless a defensible ad-valorem equivalent is available.

## 7. Integrated literature review: what the previous work tells us

### Export survival — known, not novel by itself

Nicita, Shirotori and Tumurchudur Klok (2013) explicitly included Bangladesh among 17 LDC exporters, using product-destination spells over 1993–2007 and reporting 46,770 Bangladesh spells. Their mean duration was 2.43 years and median duration one year. They used Kaplan–Meier and extended Cox methods, with particular treatment of reporting gaps, left censoring and right censoring. A recent World Bank Bangladesh Country Economic Memorandum also analyses exporter dynamics/survival using customs data for 2005–2016. A thesis that merely updates the Bangladesh survival period would offer limited novelty.

International literature establishes the methodological and substantive foundation: Besedeš and Prusa (2006) on duration and negative duration dependence; Brenton, Saborowski and von Uexkull (2010) on developing-country export-flow survival and unobserved heterogeneity; Hess and Persson (2011) on discrete-time duration models; Carrère and Strauss-Kahn (2017) on export experience; Córcoles et al. (2015) and Díaz-Mora et al. (2018) on GVCs and export stability; Socrates et al. (2020) on Kenya and AGOA; Esteve-Pérez (2021) on experience and experimentation; Fanelli, Hallak and Yin (2026) on exporter survival under uncertainty.

Fugazza and McLaren (2014) are especially relevant because they connect relative market access to firm export performance and survival. Sytsma (2021) demonstrates that rules-of-origin provisions can affect preference utilisation among LDC exporters. These findings support the general mechanism but do not themselves establish a Bangladesh-specific product-level effect under the 2027 EU GSP regime.

### Bangladesh-specific trade research

Ginting, Razzaque and Hasan (2024) study export diversification and policy impediments in Bangladesh. Bari and Jayanthakumaran (2021) examine networks, human capital and export success with Bangladeshi firm-level data. Ullah and Inaba (2012) examine RTAs/PTAs and Bangladesh's exports using gravity. Existing Bangladesh digital-service and services-competitiveness work through 2026 also makes a broad digital services topic less obviously novel than previously assumed.

### Research implication

A potentially defensible contribution is not simply “LDC graduation reduces exports” or “Bangladesh export survival is low.” It is a carefully measured, product-level evaluation of future EU market-access exposure, with statutory denominator calculations, competitor comparison, tariff-line rates and rules-of-origin conditions—if these can be assembled. Otherwise, pivot to anti-export bias or product sophistication using more reliable public data.

## 8. Provisional topic decision

**Keep as a working direction:** Bangladesh's product-level exposure to changes in EU GSP market access and the Section S-11b safeguard. Avoid a causal “impact” title unless an actual policy change and valid control group support causal identification. Because Bangladesh's LDC graduation date has been extended and EBA applies through the transition, a near-term before/after design is not appropriate. A scenario/exposure analysis is more defensible unless a quasi-experimental design emerges.

**Fallback 1:** Import tariff protection and export competitiveness in Bangladesh (anti-export bias), using a product-input tariff exposure measure and input-output concordance.

**Fallback 2:** Export diversification and product sophistication in Bangladesh, using UN Comtrade, dynamic RCA, HHI, product-space/complexity measures and destination diversification.
