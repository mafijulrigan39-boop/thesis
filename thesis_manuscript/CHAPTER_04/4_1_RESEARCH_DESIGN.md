# CHAPTER 4: RESEARCH METHODS

## 4.1 Research Design

The present study adopts a **quantitative, retrospective, observational research design** based on secondary international merchandise-trade statistics, source-dated customs tariff information, and official legal instruments. Its principal purpose is to describe Bangladesh's product-level position in the European Union (EU) import market and assess the **conditional exposure** of that position to alternative tariff-preference arrangements following Bangladesh's eventual transition from least developed country (LDC)-based preferences. The study is primarily **descriptive and comparative**, supplemented by exploratory statistical contrasts and **deterministic, fixed-trade-value tariff-scenario calculations**. It is not an experiment, a causal policy evaluation, a structural-gravity estimation, or a behavioural simulation of future export losses. This distinction follows the principle that the choice of quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods should arise from the research questions and the evidence required to answer them, rather than from a predetermined catalogue of techniques (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

### 4.1.1 Methodological Orientation and Justification

The research questions defined in Chapter 1 principally concern **measurable trade values, shares, product distributions, relative tariff conditions, and legally specified numerical thresholds**. These constructs can be operationalized using administrative statistical records and documented tariff schedules. A quantitative approach is therefore appropriate because it permits transparent calculation, cross-product comparison, sensitivity analysis, and independent reproduction of the measured indicators. The theoretical framework in Chapter 3 provides the rationale for considering relative trade costs and alternative suppliers; it does not imply that every theoretically plausible relationship can be causally identified using the available observations (Anderson & van Wincoop, 2003; Cipollina et al., 2017).

The study is **retrospective** in that its central trade measurements refer to already recorded **2025** EU imports. It is **cross-sectional** at the principal product level because the core analysis uses the native **2025 Combined Nomenclature eight-digit (CN8)** classification, rather than a source-certified, harmonized long-run panel of the same products. It is **comparative** because it examines Bangladesh alongside six selected competing countries supplying the same EU product markets. It is also **prospective only in a conditional accounting sense**: alternative tariff rates are applied to historical recorded trade values to describe possible statutory exposure under explicit assumptions. No post-EBA Bangladeshi shipment outcome is observed.

An important distinction concerns the term **analytical**. In this thesis it refers to the examination of patterns, distributions, conditional tariff differences, and exploratory relationships. It does **not** denote an explanatory causal design. The distinction matters because statistical associations and policy counterfactuals have different identification requirements from direct descriptions of a recorded population or selected cohort. As Athey and Imbens (2017) emphasize, credible causal policy evaluation depends on an explicit identification strategy; the mere presence of a quantitative model or statistically significant coefficient is insufficient. No such identification strategy is claimed here.

### 4.1.2 Evidence Structure, Reference Periods, and Units of Analysis

The empirical design integrates **three distinct evidence streams**, each retained with its own temporal and legal provenance.

The first comprises **EU-reported extra-EU merchandise imports in 2025** from Eurostat Comext, dataset **DS-045409**, with the statistical reporter defined as **EU27_2020**. Bangladesh is the origin of primary interest, and the selected product cohort consists of the **200 CN8 lines recording the highest 2025 EU import values from Bangladesh**. The principal product-level indicators include EU-recorded import value, product-specific Bangladesh market share calculated against the official **all-extra-EU-origin** import total, sector composition, and concentration within the selected 200-product basket. For matched comparisons, the unit becomes **CN8 product × supplying origin**, with China, India, Viet Nam, Pakistan, Cambodia, and Türkiye as the six specified comparators. Valid net-mass observations permit descriptive EUR-per-kilogram unit values; such measures do not directly establish quality, cost, or exporter productivity. Eurostat (2025) provides the statistical basis for distinguishing import-side valuation, origin-country designation, and the eight-digit nomenclature. The data are **EU imports originating in Bangladesh**, not independently verified Bangladesh-reported free-on-board exports.

The second stream consists of **EU tariff-policy reference information dated 1 November 2026**. It contains source-qualified most-favoured-nation (MFN) duty candidates and conditional origin- or regime-specific tariff candidates for relevant goods. These information records are matched to the selected CN8 cohort with attention to tariff-line descendants, duty type, country eligibility, rules of origin, and product-specific legal conditions. Because 2026 tariff rates are associated analytically with **earlier 2025 trade observations**, the study does not treat these rates as an observed contemporaneous intervention or as duties actually paid on the 2025 shipments. Work on EU preferential arrangements shows why nominal tariff margins and actual preference utilisation require separate interpretation (Cipollina et al., 2017; Sytsma, 2021).

The third stream comprises **2023–2025** EU import observations for the relevant textile and apparel product grouping, operationalized through **Harmonized System Chapters 61–63**, together with the pertinent conditions of **Article 33 of Regulation (EU) 2026/1395**. The historical safeguard exercise evaluates separate numerical ratios involving imports from the specified GSP beneficiary grouping and imports from all relevant extra-EU origins. Applying the **2027 beneficiary roster** retrospectively to 2023–2025 data is expressly a *roster-based numerical backcast*, not a claim about the beneficiary list legally in force in each historical year. Moreover, the Article 33 EBA exception and implementing-act requirements prevent the arithmetic result from being equated with an actual safeguard imposed on Bangladesh (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026).

These evidence streams are **analytically linked but not pooled into one homogeneous dataset**. The product–origin cross-section, the source-dated tariff benchmark, and the chapter-level historical legal screen have different units, dates, and inferential purposes. Treating them as if they constituted a longitudinal tariff-treatment panel would misstate the design.

### 4.1.3 Alignment Between Research Questions and Research Strategies

**Table 4.1** summarizes how the study's five objectives and corresponding research questions are addressed. The detailed formulas, coverage rules, and reproducible statistical procedures are specified in Sections 4.3–4.5.

**Table 4.1. Research-design components aligned with the five research questions**

| Research question | Principal evidence and observation unit | Research strategy | Permissible inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RQ1: Product structure and market position** | Bangladesh-origin EU imports; 2025 CN8 product and the selected 200-line basket | Descriptive value rankings, official-denominator market shares, basket concentration, HS2 distribution | Observed distribution **within the selected product cohort** |
| **RQ2: Competitor comparisons** | 2025 matched CN8 × origin records for Bangladesh and six comparators | Comparative import values, common-denominator shares, valid unit-value ratios; exploratory H3 sign tests | Matched destination-product differences, **not** quality or productivity effects |
| **RQ3: Tariff structures and associations** | Source-dated 2026 EU MFN and conditional preferential tariff candidates; 2025 product market shares and HS2 | Tariff-rate distribution, conditional margin comparisons; exploratory H1 permutation and H2 rank-association analysis with sector sensitivity | Source-date tariff patterns and **noncausal** selected-sample associations |
| **RQ4: Conditional preference erosion** | Fixed 2025 Bangladesh-origin import values with specified 2026 tariff reference rates | Deterministic rate-difference accounting under alternative, legally qualified scenarios and sensitivity checks | **Static duty equivalents under assumptions**, not future duties or lost exports |
| **RQ5: Product-specific safeguards** | Historical 2023–2025 HS61–63 trade totals, specified GSP-origin roster and official legal criteria | Numerically explicit Article 33 threshold and exception screens; legal-applicability assessment | Historical conditional legal screens, **not** a safeguard actually imposed or forecast |

*Note.* RQ = research question; CN8 = eight-digit Combined Nomenclature; HS2 = two-digit Harmonized System chapter. H1–H3 are post-hoc exploratory statistical checks. RQ4 and RQ5 principally involve accounting identities and legal calculations, for which the introduction of a significance test would not, by itself, improve interpretability.

The component approach is compatible with the existing scholarly distinction between *observed trade patterns* and *modelled responses* to changing trade policies. Economy-wide and partial-equilibrium studies can estimate conditional behavioural consequences when additional model parameters and assumptions are specified (Rahman & Strutt, 2024; Bekkers & Cariola, 2025). By contrast, the present design intentionally stops at measured market positions and fixed-value tariff exposures; it does not use the results of those models as coefficients for a new, unvalidated prediction.

### 4.1.4 Sampling Logic and Boundaries of Generalization

Product selection is **purposive and value-based**. The study examines the 200 CN8 products with the highest observed 2025 Bangladesh-origin EU import values because these lines provide a transparent and policy-relevant view of the major trade flows exposed to changes in preferential treatment. This criterion supports analysis of concentration and tariff coverage among high-value lines. It does not create a probability sample, and findings from those lines cannot automatically be generalized to all products exported by Bangladesh, to firm-level outcomes, or to other destination markets. Similarly, the six comparator origins constitute a selected reference group, not the complete EU supplier population.

The design makes the selection criterion especially relevant when interpreting statistical patterns. Since products are selected according to high Bangladesh-origin import value, relationships among their market shares, tariff rates, and unit values may differ from those found in smaller-value or omitted products. The study therefore reports exploratory tests only as supplementary examinations of patterns **within the selected cohort**. Their probability values are not treated as confirmation of a randomly sampled national product-population hypothesis, and no claim is made that post-hoc specification of tests constitutes preregistered inference (Athey & Imbens, 2017). The source and sampling rules, including treatment of missing trade records, are documented more fully in Section 4.4.

### 4.1.5 Conditional Scenario Analysis and Causal-Inference Limits

Prospective tariff exposure is examined through **transparent counterfactual accounting**, not through a forecast model. In each defined scenario, a source-qualified tariff-rate difference is applied to a fixed **2025 EU import-value baseline** for the products with suitable numerical and legal coverage. This approach allows the potential nominal tariff gap to be expressed in euros under a stated benchmark and permits sensitivity analysis over product coverage, sectors, and legally possible rate assumptions. It does **not** estimate how import quantities, negotiated prices, exporter margins, employment, or welfare would change if a future tariff were introduced. Such responses depend on substitution, incidence, demand and production relationships that are outside this fixed-value design (Bekkers & Cariola, 2025; Rahman & Strutt, 2024).

The future EU market-access regime is itself uncertain. The United Nations General Assembly (2026) extended Bangladesh's preparatory period before graduation to **24 November 2029**, while the EU's revised GSP legislation specifies a separate transition procedure associated with eventual removal of EBA eligibility (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026). The indicative post-EBA horizon around **2032** must accordingly be distinguished from an established future tariff-effective date or an approved successor preference regime. Therefore, scenarios using MFN, Standard GSP-like, GSP+-like, or special safeguard rate candidates are **conditional reference exercises**, not determinations that any one set of rates will legally apply to Bangladesh after graduation.

An ordinary cross-sectional regression of 2025 market share on 2026 duty candidates would not resolve this problem. The tariff variable is dated after the observed trade outcome; product characteristics and sector composition can be confounding influences; and Bangladesh has not yet experienced the policy event under investigation. As a result, even an apparently precise regression coefficient would not identify the effect of a future EBA withdrawal merely by including conventional controls. The study confines its statistical tests to the exploratory comparisons already defined in Chapter 1 and reports sensitivity to sector composition and missing data, consistent with methodological emphasis on credible identification in applied policy evaluation (Athey & Imbens, 2017).

### 4.1.6 Role of Legal Documents, Exclusion of Unused Methods, and Research Integrity

Official legislation and administrative guidance are essential inputs because they define what counts as a legally available tariff candidate, an eligible beneficiary, or a safeguard-related numerical condition. However, **using legal documents to establish documentary facts is not equivalent to conducting an independent qualitative research strand**. The present study does not use research interviews, focus groups, an ethnographic case study, survey questionnaires, or thematic coding of participant narratives. It therefore does **not** claim a mixed-methods design. A separate qualitative component could provide future insight into exporter decision-making, but introducing one here without actually collecting and analysing such evidence would misrepresent the methods employed (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

For similar reasons, structural equation modelling (SEM), survey-scale reliability assessment, and a causal multivariate regression are not included merely because they appear as examples in the institutional methodology outline. These techniques address other types of questions and require appropriate data structures, measurement instruments, or identifying conditions. Section 4.2 will state the non-applicability of qualitative data-collection procedures under the adopted design; Section 4.3 will explain the **descriptive statistics and exploratory permutation, correlation, and matched-sign procedures that were actually implemented**. Sections 4.4 and 4.5 will identify the full sample construction, primary source files, data exclusions, algorithmic steps, and independent verification checks.

### 4.1.7 Reliability, Reproducibility, and Research Validity

The validity of this design rests primarily on **measurement fidelity, source provenance, analytic transparency, and restraint in interpretation**. The source pipeline uses official trade, tariff, and legal records with defined publication and effective dates. The frozen computational analysis includes input-file fingerprints and independently repeated numerical checks. These procedures support the **computational reproducibility** of the current findings, but they do not independently certify product-specific preference utilisation, the future tariff status of Bangladesh, or causal identification. A reproducible calculation can still be an imperfect measure of an economic concept if its legal qualification, product mapping, or denominator is wrong.

The study therefore distinguishes genuinely observed zeros from unavailable values; documents CN8-to-TARIC mapping problems and complex or non-ad valorem rates; reports the count and import value of observations excluded from each scenario; and avoids calculating historical product-level growth across unreconciled classification revisions. The importance of verified concordances for longitudinal commodity comparisons is demonstrated by Baumgartner et al. (2023). Where a supplier's recorded origin coverage does not reconcile fully with an official total, concentration measures must be qualified or bounded rather than presented as exact full-market statistics. Where net mass is unavailable or invalid, the corresponding unit-value ratio is excluded rather than estimated without a documented basis.

Three boundaries summarize the research design. **First**, external validity is limited by the purposive high-value product selection, six-country comparator group, and single primary trade year. **Second**, measurement validity is limited by source-date differences, complex tariff classifications, origin conditions, and missing preference-utilisation or quantity observations. **Third**, causal validity is deliberately not claimed: the study cannot estimate a policy treatment effect because the eventual post-EBA event and the relevant identifying counterfactual are not observed. Within those constraints, a quantitative, observational and scenario-based design is suitable for answering the stated research questions and providing a reproducible description of Bangladesh's prospective EU market-access exposure.
