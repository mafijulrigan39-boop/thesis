# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## 1.6 Scope and Brief Limitations

The scope of this study is defined by its geographical coverage, product selection, reference periods, data sources, and analytical purpose. The investigation concerns Bangladesh's existing position in the European Union (EU) import market and its prospective exposure to changes in preferential market access following eventual withdrawal from the Everything But Arms (EBA) arrangement. It is designed as a product-level assessment of observed trade patterns and conditional tariff differences, rather than a forecast of the realised economic consequences of least developed country (LDC) graduation.

### 1.6.1 Scope of the Study

**Geographical and market coverage.** The study examines extra-EU merchandise imports reported for the EU-27 statistical aggregate (EU27_2020), with Bangladesh identified as the country of origin. Data are drawn primarily from Eurostat's Comext international trade in goods statistics (dataset DS-045409), which record detailed EU imports by product, partner, and reference period. In extra-EU import statistics, the partner country ordinarily denotes the country of origin rather than the country of consignment (Eurostat, 2025). Bangladesh's market position is examined alongside six selected supplying countries—China, India, Viet Nam, Pakistan, Cambodia, and Türkiye. These countries serve as comparative origins, not as an exhaustive representation of all suppliers. Accordingly, Bangladesh's share of a given EU product market is assessed against the official extra-EU import total for that product, rather than only the combined imports from these six comparators.

**Product coverage and units of analysis.** The principal empirical sample comprises the 200 eight-digit Combined Nomenclature (CN8) product lines with the highest reported EU import values originating in Bangladesh during 2025. Selection is based on observed import value and is therefore purposive rather than probabilistic. Although textiles and apparel are central to Bangladesh's EU trade, the sample is defined by value across the reported product universe rather than restricted in advance to a single industry. The main unit of observation is the CN8 product–origin combination for 2025, supplemented by product-level EU tariff-rate information. The analysis covers trade values, the composition and concentration of the selected Bangladeshi import basket, Bangladesh's share of extra-EU imports in the same product, competitor-country comparisons, and customs unit values where valid net-mass data permit consistent calculation. The statistical assessments address selected differences and associations within this observed product cohort; they do not independently measure exporter productivity or a comprehensive revealed comparative advantage index.

**Temporal and legal coverage.** The principal trade reference year is **2025**. The tariff comparisons use source-dated EU most-favoured-nation (MFN) and conditional preferential-rate benchmarks referenced to **1 November 2026**. These two reference dates are deliberately distinguished: the rates represent a subsequent policy benchmark applied analytically to observed 2025 imports, not duties shown to have been paid on those earlier transactions. Separate annual observations for **2023–2025** are used for historical numerical screening of the EU's textile and apparel safeguard provisions. Available data for **2012–2025** provide broader historical context at the Harmonized System two-digit (HS2) chapter level, particularly Chapters 61–63, but are not treated as a fully harmonized longitudinal CN8 product panel. This distinction is necessary because the EU's eight-digit commodity classification is revised annually (Eurostat, 2025; Baumgartner et al., 2023).

**Policy and analytical coverage.** The policy analysis focuses on the EU's Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP), EBA treatment, and explicitly conditional alternatives to EBA access, including illustrative MFN and other preferential-rate benchmarks where the relevant product and legal assumptions can be stated. The regulatory context takes account of Bangladesh's scheduled LDC graduation on **24 November 2029**, following the United Nations General Assembly's 2026 extension, and the separate transitional mechanism governing eventual removal from EBA under Regulation (EU) 2026/1395 (United Nations General Assembly, 2026; European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026). The resulting indicative EBA transition horizon around 2032 is conditional, not an established date on which a particular future duty will become payable. The study also examines Article 33 and the relevant annexes of the 2026 EU regulation through historically dated numerical comparisons, while recognising that the safeguard mechanism has distinct denominators, legal prerequisites, and an exception for EBA beneficiaries (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026).

### 1.6.2 Brief Limitations of the Study

**Sample representativeness and data comparability.** The focus on the 200 highest-value CN8 products provides detail on Bangladesh's leading EU import lines but excludes smaller products and does not support unqualified generalisations to the full national export basket. The six comparator countries likewise provide selected competitive benchmarks rather than a complete census of foreign suppliers. Moreover, EU import values are recorded using an import-side valuation basis, whereas Bangladesh's export statistics may follow different valuation, timing, classification, or reporting conventions; the two sources should not be assumed to match exactly (Eurostat, 2025). Where product-level quantity observations are unavailable or non-comparable, customs unit values cannot be calculated reliably. Even when available, such unit values reflect the recorded customs value relative to net mass and cannot by themselves establish product quality, factory-gate prices, or production efficiency (Gervais, 2015).

**Classification and tariff-data constraints.** Changes in CN8 classifications can create apparent product entry, exit, or growth unless successive annual code versions are reconciled using authenticated correspondence tables. As the complete 2012–2025 CN8 concordance has not been certified for this study, long-run changes in individual CN8 lines are outside its validated inferential scope (Baumgartner et al., 2023). The tariff analysis also depends on product-specific legal conditions and on the availability of usable numerical rates. Missing or unverified duties, specific or mixed tariffs, rules-of-origin requirements, and conditional preferences cannot automatically be represented by a zero rate or by a universal ad valorem equivalent. The study therefore distinguishes qualified tariff observations from excluded or illustrative assumptions and documents the resulting analytical coverage.

**Uncertainty concerning future preferences and their use.** The tariff arrangements that will apply following an eventual EBA transition have not yet been conclusively established at the product and transaction level. Eligibility for alternative preferential schemes, origin compliance, possible safeguards, subsequent EU legal acts, and the structure of competing suppliers' preferences may change. The available data also do not establish the actual 2025 preference-utilisation rate separately for every selected CN8 product and origin. Evidence from the EU rules-of-origin literature shows why nominal preference eligibility should not be equated with universal utilisation (Sytsma, 2021). Consequently, calculations based on 2026 tariff benchmarks and fixed 2025 trade values are interpreted as **conditional static duty equivalents**, not forecasts of 2029 or 2032 customs liabilities, export losses, or welfare changes. Historical Article 33 calculations using a later beneficiary-country roster are similarly illustrative backcasts and cannot demonstrate actual or future safeguard applicability.

**Limits of statistical and causal inference.** The core product-level evidence is observational and cross-sectional, and no realised post-EBA tariff withdrawal is observed in the sample. The statistical hypotheses were developed after preliminary examination of the dataset and are treated as exploratory; their results may also be sensitive to sectoral composition and purposive product selection. Accordingly, the analysis cannot identify the causal effect of tariff-preference erosion on export volumes, prices, employment, wages, firm profitability, or economic welfare. Such outcomes would require additional behavioural assumptions or appropriately designed post-policy observations. To limit overinterpretation, the study reports source dates, valid-data coverage, missingness, sectoral and product-group sensitivities, and the boundaries between descriptive evidence, exploratory inference, and conditional legal or accounting assessments.
