# Bangladesh EU market-access thesis — preliminary data validation audit

**Evidence cut-off:** 17 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Validate whether a product-level thesis on Bangladesh's future EU market-access exposure can be supported by public secondary data.  
**Decision:** **Do not finalise the thesis title yet.** A 2024 trade-value screen passes, but the legally decisive safeguard denominator, complete product-time series and tariff-line panel are still unvalidated.

## 1. Updated policy baseline

A material policy development changes the original design. UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/81/8, adopted on 29 October 2026, extends Bangladesh's preparatory period preceding LDC graduation until **24 November 2029**. The earlier 2026 graduation date must not be used as the current baseline.

The EU's new GSP Regulation (EU) 2026/1395 was published on 22 June 2026 and applies from 1 January 2027. Under Article 17(2), an EBA beneficiary that no longer fulfils the LDC condition is removed from EBA following a three-year transition from the date it ceases to qualify as an LDC. On the present UN date, this points to an EBA transition potentially extending roughly to late November 2032, but the exact end date must be confirmed by the Commission's delegated act and country-status notice.

Article 18 suspends Common Customs Tariff duties on products originating in an EBA beneficiary in CN Chapters 1–97, except Chapter 93. Article 7(2) provides that ad valorem duties for sensitive products in GSP sections S-11a and S-11b are reduced by **20%** under Standard GSP. This is a relative reduction, not a 20-percentage-point reduction. Article 12 provides suspension of ad valorem duties for products listed in Annexes III and VII originating in a GSP+ beneficiary country. Bangladesh's eligibility for GSP+ cannot be assumed; it requires a separate assessment of vulnerability criteria, conventions, reporting, effective implementation and Commission approval.

The most specific and potentially important rule is Article 33. GSP Section S-11b comprises Chapters 61, 62 and 63. For a beneficiary country, the Commission may remove the Article 7 / Article 12 preferences where that country's imports in the relevant S-11b section exceed **37% of EU imports of those products from all GSP beneficiary countries** during the relevant calendar year. Article 33(2) excludes EBA beneficiaries and countries whose share of total EU imports of the relevant products does not exceed 6%. The 37% denominator and the 6% exemption use different bases. The statutory test is section-wide, not a test on one HS-6 line or on Bangladesh's share of global imports in Chapters 61 and 62.

## 2. What has been collected and calculated

A 2024 product-level market screen was assembled from public WITS pages presenting UN Comtrade data. It includes:
- EU imports for HS Chapters 61 and 62 in 2024;
- Bangladesh-origin imports in those chapters;
- ten selected, high-value HS-6 apparel product lines;
- Bangladesh value and the top three competitor values where visible in the WITS pages;
- a selected partner sample for HS 610910.

All trade values in the source rows are **US$ thousands**. These are a screening snapshot, not a downloaded 2012–2025 panel and not a replacement for the final Eurostat Comext extraction.

The ten selected lines sum to US$11.939bn of Bangladesh's US$19.785bn imports in Chapters 61–62, or **60.34%** of Bangladesh's value in those two chapters. This shows that a tractable HS-6 sample can capture a substantial portion of the relevant trade basket. It is not a complete product census: the ten lines were selected as large, illustrative products.

Examples:
- HS 610910: EU imports were US$6.997bn; Bangladesh supplied US$3.489bn, approximately 49.86% of the global-source total. Türkiye was the next supplier at US$1.056bn; India supplied US$653m.
- HS 620342: Bangladesh supplied US$2.078bn of US$5.662bn; Pakistan supplied US$947m and Türkiye US$665m.
- HS 610462: Bangladesh supplied about 42.04% of the EU import value in this product line.

The observed concentration makes the issue worth investigating. It does **not** prove that the Article 33 safeguard threshold is triggered, and it does not prove that Bangladesh will be granted GSP+ or Standard GSP after EBA.

## 3. Legal tariff scenarios to test

| Scenario | Tariff treatment | What needs to be extracted |
|---|---|---|
| EBA baseline | Duty suspension for almost all goods, subject to origin and customs requirements | Confirm current EBA treatment by tariff line and date |
| Standard GSP | Sensitive textile ad valorem duties reduced by 20% under S-11a/S-11b, subject to Article 7(3), product list and safeguards | Exact CN8/CN10 MFN rate, any specific component, prior preferential rate and Article 8 / Article 33 suspension |
| GSP+ | Ad valorem duties suspended for eligible products in Annexes III and VII | Product line eligibility, specific-duty treatment and Bangladesh's actual GSP+ status |
| MFN stress test | Normal Common Customs Tariff | Current and historical CN8/CN10 tariff lines by effective date |

The Standard GSP 20% textile reduction must be applied to the relevant ad valorem MFN rate. Example only: if the applicable MFN ad valorem rate were 12%, a 20% relative reduction would result in 9.6%, not 0%. The actual tariff rate for each CN8/CN10 item was **not** extracted in this audit.

## 4. Data inventory and actual access status

### Eurostat Comext — primary dataset

Dataset: **DS-045409 — EU trade since 1988 by HS2-4-6 and CN8**. Eurostat documents detailed HS6/CN8 trade by partner, monthly and annual, with annual and monthly CSV bulk files from 1988 up to the latest reference month. It supports the required reporter/partner/flow/product/year/value/quantity structure. The Comext API uses a dedicated endpoint and does not permit an unfiltered full-dataset download because of dataset size; filtered queries are required. 

The public database and bulk-download documentation were verified. A complete filtered panel was not downloaded in this execution environment. The API was not accessible to the current retrieval route. Therefore the full series is an outstanding collection step, not part of the attached data.

**Recommended extraction filters:**
- Reporter: EU27_2020 (or the exact aggregate reporter code used in the selected release);
- Flow: imports;
- Partner: Bangladesh plus all non-EU partners; for the safeguard test, all GSP beneficiary origins are needed;
- Product: Chapters 61, 62 and 63, using annual HS6/CN8 product dimensions;
- Period: 2012–2025 initially, then test 2002–2025 where classification coverage permits;
- Indicators: value in euros, net mass/quantity, supplementary quantity where available.

Use the matching Eurostat nomenclature/concordance files and aggregate CN8 tariff lines to a stable HS6 or CN chapter level. Do not directly concatenate year-specific CN8 codes without concordance.

### WITS / UN Comtrade — screening source

WITS provided 2024 partner-level product pages and the values transcribed in the workbook/CSV. Use these only as a cross-check and for initial screening. Final analysis should use one consistent primary series, ideally Comext values in euros, or a carefully documented Comtrade reporter/partner specification. Do not mix EU Comext and mirror-reported Bangladesh exports without reconciling valuation, partner, flow and revision differences.

### Tariff data

The WTO Integrated Database (IDB) contains detailed applied and preferential tariffs at national tariff-line level from 1996, but WTO documentation says IDB downloads are accessible only to WTO member governments and authorised international organisations. It is therefore not safe to design this master's thesis around public access to IDB raw downloads.

Prioritise EU TARIC / Access2Markets tariff-line extraction. WITS / UNCTAD TRAINS are supplementary options, but coverage, update year and HS revision need testing. Exact CN8/CN10 rates have not yet been downloaded and validated.

### Preference utilisation

The distinction between eligibility, applicable preference, origin compliance and actual preference utilisation is essential. Aggregate EU statistics may show overall utilisation rates, but this audit has not obtained a public, complete HS6-by-country utilisation dataset. Do not use eligibility as a proxy for utilisation or assert that every eligible shipment actually entered under the preference.

## 5. Article 33 safeguard calculation — exact steps

To test the possible post-EBA S-11b safeguard exposure for Bangladesh:

1. Transcribe the beneficiary-country list and status (Standard GSP, GSP+ or EBA) from the applicable Annex I of Regulation 2026/1395 and the Commission's effective list. Maintain year-specific flags; include only actual beneficiaries in the statutory denominator and check any temporary suspensions.
2. Retrieve EU imports by partner origin and all products in S-11b: Chapters 61, 62 and 63, ideally at CN8 and then aggregate consistently.
3. For each calendar year, calculate:
   - Bangladesh S-11b value;
   - total EU S-11b imports from all GSP beneficiary countries;
   - Bangladesh share of the beneficiary-country denominator;
   - Bangladesh share of total EU S-11b imports from all origins.
4. Compare the first ratio with 37% and the second ratio with the 6% exemption threshold.
5. Model Bangladesh both as the present EBA beneficiary (Article 33 exemption applies) and as a *hypothetical* post-EBA GSP/GSP+ beneficiary. Do not pre-label future country status.
6. Keep Standard GSP Article 8 product graduation separate: its method uses three consecutive years and distinct thresholds in Annex IV; do not mix it with the one-calendar-year Article 33 S-11b test.

## 7. Preliminary conclusion and title decision

The trade screen supports the academic relevance of a Bangladesh–EU apparel market-access project. Bangladesh has a substantial EU presence in Chapters 61 and 62, and the new EU GSP law creates a specific statutory safeguard framework that a generic tariff-preference-margin study would miss.

However, the research question may be more interesting and distinctive if it examines **post-EBA exposure to the EU's S-11b safeguard mechanism**, rather than only average preference erosion. This is still a provisional research direction, not a final title. It depends on whether a reproducible full Section S-11b import panel and tariff-line data can be assembled.

Do not finalise the title until all three of these go/no-go checks pass:
- the full all-beneficiary S-11b country denominator can be calculated for multiple years;
- the 6% total-EU-import exemption is measured with Chapter 63 included;
- CN8/CN10 tariff lines and the actual Standard GSP/GSP+ alternatives can be mapped to the major Bangladesh export products.

If these fail, pivot to a more feasible topic using product diversification and sophistication or tariff protection/anti-export bias rather than forcing a safeguard thesis.
