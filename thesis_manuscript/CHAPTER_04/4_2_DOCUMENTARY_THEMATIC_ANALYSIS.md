# CHAPTER 4: RESEARCH METHODS

## 4.2 Methodological Scope and Documentary Thematic Analysis

Although this thesis is predominantly quantitative, a bounded form of **qualitative documentary analysis** is appropriate because its tariff-exposure scenarios and Article 33 screens depend on the interpretation of official legal and policy texts. These sources specify the conditions under which a country or product may qualify for preferential treatment, the timing of eligibility transitions, the legal population used in safeguard thresholds, and the administrative acts necessary before a measure applies. Such provisions are not adequately represented by a numeric rate alone. The study therefore incorporates **directed qualitative content analysis with deductive thematic categorisation** of a small, purposefully selected body of official documents. This is an ancillary **legal-interpretation and variable-qualification procedure** within a quantitative-dominant research design, rather than an independent interview-based qualitative enquiry.

Bowen (2009) identifies documentary analysis as a systematic approach to reviewing records as research evidence, while Hsieh and Shannon (2005) distinguish *directed content analysis*—where a conceptual or theoretical framework guides initial coding—from conventional inductive and summative approaches. These methodological distinctions fit the present task because the relevant categories are specified in advance by Chapter 3's variable framework and the legal questions arising from Article 17, Article 33, and the EU's rules of preferential origin. Braun and Clarke (2006) establish the broader possibilities of thematic analysis; however, the present procedure does **not** claim to implement a full reflexive, inductively developed thematic analysis of participant accounts. It uses pre-established codes to classify documentary provisions whose meaning must remain anchored in their authoritative source text.

### 4.2.1 Rationale, Purpose, and Relationship to the Quantitative Design

The documentary component has a narrowly defined purpose: to make the legal assumptions of the quantitative analysis **explicit, traceable, and consistent**. For instance, a tariff candidate cannot be treated as automatically applicable merely because a country appears in the appropriate preferential arrangement; the relevant product, beneficiary status, rules of origin, and specific conditions must also be satisfied (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026, arts. 4, 7, 9–10, 17–18, and 39). Similarly, Article 33 requires the analyst to distinguish the relevant GSP-beneficiary import denominator from the separate all-Union-import denominator and to account for the EBA exemption and implementing-act procedure. The resulting legal classifications help define the variables used in RQ3–RQ5, but they are not observations of future exporter behaviour or causal treatment effects.

The procedure is *deductive* because categories are derived from the existing theory-to-construct framework and the research questions rather than sought as emergent social themes. It is *documentary* because the evidence consists of authoritative published texts rather than respondent statements. It is *interpretive but constrained* because a theme label cannot override the actual wording, hierarchy, applicability date, or status of a legal provision. Importantly, no claim is made that the frequency of a theme's appearance measures its legal importance or economic significance. This approach reflects Hsieh and Shannon's (2005) rationale for using established knowledge to guide text coding, while recognizing Bowen's (2009) emphasis on document authenticity, relevance, and critical evaluation.

### 4.2.2 Documentary Corpus and Selection Criteria

Documents were selected **purposively**, according to their direct bearing on the study's research questions, institutional authority, current relevance, and availability of verifiable source locations. The core documentary corpus comprises **three official source families**:

1. **EU legal framework:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1395, including the articles governing Standard GSP, GSP+, EBA, preferential origin, the special safeguards, and Annexes I, III, and IV (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026). This is the principal normative source for the coded eligibility and safeguard rules; the regulation is scheduled to apply from **1 January 2027**.
2. **UN graduation-status authority:** United Nations General Assembly Resolution A/RES/81/8 and the UN's official LDC graduation-status record for Bangladesh, establishing the extended graduation timetable to **24 November 2029** (United Nations General Assembly, 2026). These establish the country-status reference date but not the future EU product tariff rates.
3. **EU institutional implementation evidence:** The European Commission and High Representative's joint report on the GSP for **2023–2025**, used to distinguish the concept of *preference utilisation* from eligibility for preferential treatment (European Commission & High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, 2026). The joint report provides contextual administrative evidence; it does not substitute for the regulation or supply observed 2025 CN8-level claims for every selected product.

The *unit of documentary coding* is a discrete legally meaningful provision or identifiable official-report statement, rather than an entire document or a count of keywords. Multiple clauses may be grouped into one evidence unit only when their joint interpretation is needed to establish a rule, for example a safeguard threshold with its stated exemption. The accompanying source ledger records the instrument, article or identified report location, predefined code, analytical interpretation, linked research question, and limitation. The corpus is deliberately focused on **documented legal eligibility and data interpretation**, not on an exhaustive investigation of Bangladesh's domestic policy or of every conceivable EU customs instrument. The full product-level TARIC measures and origin-specific conditions are handled by the separate tariff-data pipeline in Sections 4.4–4.5.

### 4.2.3 Deductive Coding Procedure and Documentary Themes

The coding followed six predefined themes derived from the constructs in Section 3.2. First, the original regulatory and policy sources were read by provision and their legal authority and effective dates were distinguished. Second, material relevant to the five research questions was assigned to one of the predefined codes, with its article or source locator retained. Third, each coded passage was paraphrased conservatively into an operational rule or caveat. Fourth, that rule was matched to the quantitative variable or scenario whose interpretation it constrained. Fifth, conflicting dates, contingent eligibility, exemptions, and other qualifications were retained as explicit exceptions rather than simplified into a single tariff status. This is a **directed coding workflow**, not a claim of saturation, intercoder agreement, or inductive discovery of new categories (Hsieh & Shannon, 2005).

The structured provision-level evidence ledger is kept as an accompanying, versioned methodological appendix. Theme T6 is a documentary validity control and does not imply a new time-series estimator.

### 4.2.4 Integration with Quantitative Measures and a Traceable Audit Trail

The documentary themes are applied at specific decision points in the quantitative workflow. **T1–T3** guide the interpretation of tariff-rate scenarios: a candidate is classified by product, origin, scheme, tariff type, and known qualification status, while unverified application conditions remain explicitly unresolved. **T2** keeps the 2029 UN graduation decision separate from the conditional EU EBA transition around 2032; it does not create a known future rate. **T4–T5** specify the two noninterchangeable Article 33 ratios and identify when the safeguard mechanism does not apply. **T6** enforces temporal and beneficiary-roster disclosure so that a historical calculation using a 2027 roster is not described as an operative 2023–2025 legal test.

This form of integration is **interpretive and procedural**, not an independently estimated qualitative outcome. The coding does not alter observed 2025 EU import values, assign missing TARIC rates, produce a new country-risk score, or make an unobserved tariff preference legally applicable. Instead, it establishes restrictions and labels that must accompany numerical calculations. Where the text specifies conditions not verifiable from the available product-level data, the respective tariff treatment is retained as conditional or unavailable. In this sense, the documentary stage increases **construct validity** by limiting the claims attached to the calculated variables, not by adding a second statistical estimator.

The source matrix is designed to be inspectable: each entry contains a documentary source, a specific legal or report locator, a controlled theme code, a conservative interpretation, its link to RQ3–RQ5, and an express qualification. The source document is distinguished from the researcher's interpretation. All legal interpretations remain subject to a final check against the version of EU legislation operative at the analysis date, including any delegated or implementing acts. A single-researcher provision-level coding matrix is a transparency mechanism; it is **not** evidence of independent second-coder confirmation, thematic saturation, or formal inter-rater reliability.

### 4.2.5 Procedures Not Undertaken and Methodological Limitations

The study **does not conduct interviews, focus groups, field observations, ethnography, or qualitative case studies of particular exporting firms**. It does not collect participant narratives, survey exporter expectations, or employ open-ended inductive coding designed to derive new themes concerning firm experiences. Consequently, there are no respondents, interview recordings, transcripts, participant-sampling methods, or participant consent records to report. A claim that such methods were undertaken would be inaccurate. Thematic analysis, in the broader sense discussed by Braun and Clarke (2006), could be informative in a separate future investigation of exporters' responses to market-access uncertainty; it is not used here to infer unobserved behavioural mechanisms.

The documentary content analysis is subject to several limitations. The legal corpus is **purposive and small**, and its themes are specified in advance, so it is designed for **focused classification rather than discovery or exhaustive legal scholarship**. Certain product-level questions still require further verification of TARIC ten-digit measures, rules-of-origin evidence, cumulation rules, country-specific arrangements, and subsequent Commission acts. Moreover, the EU's legal framework can change, and the economic effects of a tariff depend on behaviours not established by the legal text. Documents that report aggregate preference utilisation cannot be used to infer which of the study's individual 2025 CN8 imports actually claimed a preference. These limitations prevent the researcher from presenting the thematic categories as a direct measure of commercial outcomes.

Finally, the status of the design should be expressed precisely. The study is **quantitative-dominant with ancillary, directed documentary content analysis**. The documentary exercise is integrated as an audit of the legal conditions governing quantitative variables, and it does not constitute an independently sampled or interpreted qualitative strand aimed at answering a separate research question. It is therefore not presented as a balanced explanatory-sequential or convergent mixed-methods investigation merely because documents are interpreted alongside numerical data (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Whether the department prefers the broader label *embedded mixed methods* for this ancillary legal procedure is a matter for supervisor approval; either choice must describe the same actual procedures rather than imply interviews or qualitative findings that do not exist.

### 4.2.6 Methodological Contribution to the Study

The limited documentary analysis serves an important but bounded function: it converts complex institutional provisions into **auditable qualifications for the tariff and safeguard variables** without conflating legal status and economic impact. The resulting themes support the source-dated validity of the conditional tariff-rate comparisons (RQ3), the interpretation of illustrative post-EBA duty-equivalent scenarios (RQ4), and the correct denominator, exemption, and implementing-act conditions for Article 33 screening (RQ5). They do not modify the observational basis of RQ1–RQ2, provide a new causal counterfactual, or resolve the absence of transaction-level preference-use records.

Accordingly, the qualitative techniques mentioned in the original thesis outline are not adopted indiscriminately: **interviews and exporter case studies are inapplicable to the data actually collected**, whereas a *focused deductive documentary coding procedure* is justified by the legal-data integration problem. The next sections set out the quantitative measurements and executed statistical tools (Section 4.3), the sample and versioned data sources (Section 4.4), and the precise computational and source-validation steps (Section 4.5).
