# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## 1.4 Research Questions and Hypotheses

The research questions are derived from the general and specific objectives of the study and are framed to distinguish observed export-market characteristics from conditional future market-access exposure. The investigation combines questions that can be addressed through descriptive product-level evidence with a limited set of exploratory statistical hypotheses. This distinction is necessary because the available trade observations precede Bangladesh's eventual withdrawal from the European Union's (EU) Everything But Arms (EBA) arrangement; hence, neither observed post-EBA outcomes nor the causal effects of preference withdrawal are available for direct estimation. The interpretation of preferential market access must also account for differences in the magnitude and utilisation of tariff preferences across products and countries (Cipollina et al., 2017; Sytsma, 2021).

### 1.4.1 Research Questions

The study seeks to address the following five research questions.

**RQ1 — Product-level export structure and market position.** How are the value, product concentration, sectoral composition, and EU extra-EU import-market shares of Bangladesh's 200 highest-value CN8 export product lines distributed in the 2025 reference year?

**RQ2 — Competitor-relative export performance.** How does Bangladesh's position in the selected EU product markets compare with that of China, India, Viet Nam, Pakistan, Cambodia, and Türkiye in terms of matched import values, product-market shares, and customs unit values where comparable quantity observations are available?

**RQ3 — Tariff-preference structure and market-share relationships.** How do source-dated EU MFN tariff-rate candidates and conditional preferential tariff advantages vary across the selected products and competing origins, and what exploratory product-level associations can be observed between those tariff-rate benchmarks and Bangladesh's 2025 EU market shares?

**RQ4 — Conditional tariff-preference erosion.** How do the static tariff-duty equivalents associated with alternative, explicitly specified post-EBA tariff scenarios vary across Bangladesh's principal EU export products, and how sensitive are the calculated exposure measures to sectoral composition, product coverage, code classifications, and missing or legally unqualified tariff observations?

**RQ5 — EU safeguard framework and conditional thresholds.** What do historical EU import data for Harmonized System Chapters 61–63 indicate when compared with the numerical thresholds in Article 33 of Regulation (EU) 2026/1395 using explicitly stated beneficiary-country assumptions, and how does the EBA exception limit the legal interpretation of these comparisons?

These five questions correspond, respectively, to the five specific objectives in Section 1.3. Questions RQ1, RQ4, and RQ5 principally concern descriptive measurement, accounting identities, and conditional legal assessments; RQ2 and RQ3 additionally motivate exploratory statistical comparisons. In particular, the Article 33 analysis distinguishes the share calculated using imports from the relevant GSP beneficiary countries from the separate 6% exception calculated using all Union imports of the relevant products. A historical calculation that applies a later beneficiary-country roster is a backcast, not a determination that the safeguard was legally applicable during those earlier years (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026, art. 33).

### 1.4.2 Exploratory Statistical Hypotheses

To supplement the research questions, the study considers three sets of testable hypotheses. Their purpose is to examine patterns within the selected product cohort, rather than to establish the effect of a future tariff shock. The motivation for distinguishing product sectors and competing suppliers follows research showing heterogeneity in EU tariff-preference effects (Cipollina et al., 2017), while the interpretation of customs unit-value comparisons remains subject to the distinction between recorded price-related measures and underlying product quality or productivity (Gervais, 2015).

**H1 — Sectoral differences in MFN tariff-rate benchmarks (related to RQ3).** Define Δ as the arithmetic mean of the 1 November 2026 EU MFN tariff-rate candidates for selected CN8 products in HS Chapters 61–63 minus the corresponding mean for selected products outside those chapters, using only lines with valid numeric rate candidates.

- **Null hypothesis (H1₀):** Δ ≤ 0. The mean MFN tariff-rate candidate for the selected apparel and related textile chapters is not higher than that for the other selected products.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H1ₐ):** Δ > 0. The mean MFN tariff-rate candidate for the selected apparel and related textile chapters is higher than that for the other selected products.

This directional comparison is assessed through an exploratory one-sided product-label permutation test. It describes differences in the selected tariff schedules; it does not measure the behavioural effect of those duties on trade.

**H2 — Product-level association between tariff rates and EU market shares (related to RQ3).** Let ρ denote the Spearman rank correlation between Bangladesh's 2025 share of extra-EU imports for a selected CN8 product and the product's matched 1 November 2026 EU MFN tariff-rate candidate.

- **Null hypothesis (H2₀):** ρ = 0. No monotonic rank association is detected between the two measured variables in the selected product cohort.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H2ₐ):** ρ ≠ 0. A nonzero monotonic rank association exists between the two measured variables in that cohort.

This two-sided exploratory test does not specify that higher tariffs improve or weaken export performance. The analysis additionally considers sector-composition sensitivity using within-HS2 rank adjustment. Since the tariff-rate candidates and import-market shares refer to different reference dates, the association must not be interpreted as a contemporaneous treatment effect or evidence that future preference erosion causes a change in EU market shares.

**H3 — Relative customs unit values across competing origins (related to RQ2).** For each comparison country *c*—China, India, Viet Nam, Pakistan, Cambodia, and Türkiye—let πc represent the proportion of valid, non-tied matched 2025 CN8 product observations in which Bangladesh's EU import customs unit value (EUR per kilogram) is lower than that of country *c*.

- **Null hypothesis (H3₀,c):** πc ≤ 0.50. Bangladesh does not exhibit a lower customs unit value in more than half of the valid non-tied product comparisons with origin *c*.
- **Alternative hypothesis (H3ₐ,c):** πc > 0.50. Bangladesh exhibits a lower customs unit value in more than half of the valid non-tied product comparisons with origin *c*.

Six origin-specific exploratory one-sided exact sign tests are considered, excluding ties and observations without valid, positive, comparable net-mass quantities. The analysis addresses multiple testing across these comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure. Relative customs unit values are interpreted only as trade-statistical indicators and not as direct estimates of production costs, export profitability, labour productivity, or product quality (Gervais, 2015).

### 1.4.3 Non-statistical Analytical Assessments

The remaining questions do not require null-hypothesis significance tests. Under **RQ4**, a static tariff-duty equivalent is obtained by applying a clearly specified percentage-point tariff-rate difference to the corresponding observed 2025 product import value and aggregating over product lines with valid, qualified rates. The calculation holds the baseline import values constant. It is therefore an accounting measure, not an estimated reduction in Bangladesh's exports, an actual future customs liability, or a welfare effect. Model-based studies of LDC graduation distinguish such baseline accounting from behavioural trade simulations that depend on substitution and preference-utilisation assumptions (Bekkers & Cariola, 2025).

Under **RQ5**, the relevant import-share ratios are calculated and examined against the numerical provisions of Article 33 and Annex IV of Regulation (EU) 2026/1395. The analysis uses historical observations and specifies when a 2027 beneficiary-country grouping is applied retrospectively. These comparisons do not themselves establish that safeguard duties were imposed, can currently be imposed on an EBA beneficiary, or will necessarily apply after Bangladesh's eventual departure from EBA (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2026).

**Status and interpretation of the hypotheses.** H1–H3 were formulated after the initial examination of the study's underlying trade and tariff data; they are therefore **post-hoc and exploratory**, rather than preregistered confirmatory hypotheses. Moreover, the product cohort is selected purposively by Bangladesh's 2025 EU import value, not drawn randomly from the full universe of exported goods. Statistical significance, if reported, is thus interpreted cautiously and cannot establish population-wide causal relationships. Detailed measurement rules, valid observation counts, test procedures, robustness assessments, and limitations are presented in Chapter 4, while the observed test results are reserved for Chapter 5.
