## B. Chapter 1 — precise source-derived instructions (PDF pp. 3–10)

### 1.1 Background of the Study (pp. 3–4)
**Required:** Define concepts and variables, establish global relevance and international policies/developments (WTO/UN/World Bank/IMF sources as applicable), narrow to Bangladesh's export context and relevant national policy, specify the EU market-access/product issue, and finish with what remains unclear and why research is justified.

**Prohibited:** Jump straight into problem without context; insert detailed methods; lengthy literature survey; unrelated history/definitions; opinions without support; excessive broad statistics.

**This thesis:** global tariff preference systems and EU import regime → LDC graduation and Bangladesh → product-level EU preference erosion/competitive exposure, anchored by correctly dated legal and trade evidence. Do not present projected loss figures in background as facts.

### 1.2 Problem Statement and Research Gap (pp. 4–6)
**Required:** Describe observed system and challenge, evidence of potential problem, what prior literature covers and misses (geographical, temporal, method, theoretical, context), and precise contribution. Critically distinguish prior general CGE/country-level analyses from descriptive CN8 product-level evidence if supported.

**Prohibited:** “No research exists” absent exhaustive proof; vague claims; emotionally criticize authors; put the study's own findings or proposed solutions here.

### 1.3 Research Objectives (pp. 6–7)
**Required:** one general purpose and a small set of specific, measurable, realistic objectives; appropriate verbs include assess, compare, measure, evaluate and identify.

**Prohibited:** generic “understand/know/learn”, recommendations masquerading as objectives, too many or disconnected objectives.

**This thesis:** measurable 2025 EU imports/market share/concentration; 2026-source product tariff candidate and conditional exposure; competitor EUR/kg and margins; Chapter 61–63 Article 33 numerical screen and its EBA exception; robustness/coverage.

### 1.4 Research Questions or Hypotheses (pp. 7–8)
**Required:** precise analytical questions aligned to objectives. Quantitative H0 and H1 must state measurable variables, expected relationship and theoretical/literature rationale.

**Prohibited:** yes/no-only and overly broad questions; vague opinions; hypotheses without measurable variables.

**This thesis:** follow *task_g/HYPOTHESES.md*. The H1–H3 tests are **post-hoc exploratory**. H2's pooled association is not robust to HS2 sector adjustment; do not assert causal impact.

### 1.5 Significance (pp. 8–9)
**Required:** grounded academic, policy, industry/management, social-economic and (where justified) methodological relevance.

**Prohibited:** exaggerated “solve all problems” claims, repeated objectives, extensive policy recommendations or promotional tone.

### 1.6 Scope and Brief Limitations (pp. 9–10)
**Required:** geography, affected product sectors, period, data unit/population/sample, measured variables and theoretical scope; honest constraints with mitigation.

**This thesis:** EU27_2020 EU imports from Bangladesh; primary *2025 top-200 CN8 cross-section*; some 2012–2025 HS2 aggregate context and non-harmonized source-year rows strictly labelled; 2026-11-01 TARIC reference; purposive high-value selection, excluded/missing rates, country residuals, policy horizon limitations. Do not claim 2012–2025 harmonized CN8 panel is certified.

### 1.7 Organization (PDF p. 10)
Briefly introduce the selected seven chapters. Ignore the five-chapter *example* if inconsistent with the primary outline. No new concepts, results or excessively detailed chapter summaries.

## C. Chapter 2 — literature review (PDF pp. 11–12)

- **2.1 Thematic review:** define preference erosion, EU GSP/EBA, tariff preferences and margins, competitiveness/product-market share, comparative advantage and related frameworks; structure around **themes**, compare different approaches and assumptions. Do not list abstract summaries author-by-author.
- **2.2 Empirical review:** compare methods, populations, data, variables and findings of Bangladesh, EU preference, rules of origin, country-level equilibrium versus product-level studies. Present contradictory evidence and limitations, not merely supportive articles.
- **2.3 Gap:** derive only gaps supported by the verified studies; be specific about time, geography, product resolution or methodological limitation and show what the **actual dataset** can fill. Do not claim a unique gap without checking.
- **Sources:** academic journals/books for theory and research; official data, legal regulations, statistical reports for context and data documentation. APA author-year in text.

## D. Chapter 3 — theoretical framework (PDF pp. 13–14)

- **3.1 Selected theory:** cite origin/developments and **justify fit**, rather than including all illustrative examples. For this subject, plausible candidate foundations *to validate against literature* include comparative advantage/export competitiveness and trade preference/market-access theory. GVC can be a secondary contextual mechanism only if useful. **OLI/FDI is not mandatory and is probably peripheral to a product-tariff focus.**
- **3.2 Variables:** define observed descriptive outcomes (market share, import value, EUR/kg), source tariff candidate/regime, product characteristics/HS2 chapter and conditional rate-difference exposure. Identify independent/dependent labels **only for actual modelled associations**; do not transform an accounting identity into causal regressors.
- **3.3 Diagram:** a simple, literature-grounded representation separating *legal tariff regime/reference rates*, *competitiveness indicators*, *tariff exposure accounting* and *sector/confounder context*. Arrows mean proposed conceptual relationships, **not identified causal paths**.
- **3.4 References:** peer-reviewed/book theoretical support for the links, with consistent APA citations.
- **Prohibited:** ornamental diagrams, unsupported directional causal assertions, unrelated theory lectures, textbook copying, unconnected variables.

## E. Chapter 4 — methodological reproducibility (PDF pp. 15–18)

- **4.1 Design:** quantitative, retrospective, observational, **secondary-data cross-sectional** design. The 2025 product-year/2026 tariff schedule is **not** an observed before-after natural experiment.
- **4.2 Qualitative examples:** interviews/case studies/thematic coding described only if actually conducted; otherwise mark not applicable or seek supervisor approval to omit. Do **not** invent respondents or ethics approvals.
- **4.3 Quantitative:** explain source-value shares, concentration, unit-value comparisons, raw tariff preference margin and conditional static duty equivalent, exact tests and permutation correlations used in Task G. A regression/SEM/survey is **not required simply because listed as an example**. Software is **Python 3.12 (standard-library script), GitHub Actions and relevant actual data processors**; do not say SPSS/STATA/R unless used.
- **4.4 Sampling/data:** define population (EU import CN8 product markets), 200 highest-value Bangladesh CN8 purposive selection in 2025, six named competitors and exact origin/year filters. Explain Eurostat Comext DS-045409, TARIC date, Annex/legal sources and gaps. Do not call selection random.
- **4.5 Analysis:** explain retrieval, input file checksums, missingness coding (NA != zero), 2025 classification flags, 11 verified hashes, numeric rate joins, origin-country panel, H1/H2/H3 exploratory tests, alternative HS2-year denominator and legal screens, and exact QA. Give units, formulas, sample denominators, significance assumptions and corrections. Never report findings in methodology. Cronbach alpha/reliability scales are for instruments where applicable; do not invent these for trade administrative data.

## F. Chapters 5–7 — outline-level rules (PDF pp. 1–2 only)

### 5 Results
5.1 Numerical descriptives (value, frequency, average, dispersion, coverage); 5.2 only **executed** inferential tests (permutation, correlation, exact sign), clearly labelled post-hoc; 5.3 actual verified tables and reproducible figures.

**Hard separation:** No literature-driven interpretation, policy recommendation, “therefore Bangladesh will lose exports”, or causal assertion in Results. Explain units and denominators as neutral table notes.

### 6 Discussion
6.1 Interpret results against each objective/hypothesis and evidence from literature; highlight **null/failed robustness**, notably HS2-adjusted H2. 6.2.1 theoretical contribution limited to what design supports; 6.2.2 cautious exporter and government implications conditional on regime applicability; 6.3 transparent data/classification, missing tariff, EBA safeguard legal and no-causality limitations plus future research.

### 7 Conclusion
Synthesize evidence, state bounded contribution and a defensible final conclusion; do not introduce new empirical claims or over-forecast.

## G. Referencing (PDF p. 2; pp. 11–15)

- **APA author–date in-text citations.** Examples in source: (Porter, 1990), (Johnson & Noguera, 2012); three-plus authors: (Dunning et al., 2018); narrative Porter (1990); direct quoted material includes page, e.g. (UNCTAD, 2022, p. 45).
- **References:** book — author/year/title/publisher; journal — author/year/article/journal/volume(issue)/pages/DOI when available; report — organization/year/title/publisher; website — organization/year/title/URL.
- Bibliography must be checked against original publisher/DOI/official body; implement corrections in *task_g/BIBLIOGRAPHY_AUDIT.md*. Do not manufacture sources, authors, dates, page numbers or direct quotations.
- **Edition and formatting** not explicit in supplied guide; choose APA 7 provisionally, and confirm final institutional style.
